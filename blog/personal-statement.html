How to Write a Winning Personal Statement

By Admissions Expert July 5, 2023 6 min read

The Power of Your Story

Your personal statement is often the only part of your application where your voice can truly shine. While your grades and test scores show your academic ability, the personal statement shows who you are, what you value, and why you are a perfect fit for the program.

"Don't just list your achievements; tell the story of the person who achieved them."

1. Start with a Hook

Admissions officers read hundreds of essays. You need to grab their attention from the first sentence. Avoid clichés like "From a young age, I've always been interested in..." Instead, start with a specific moment, a challenge you faced, or a unique observation.

2. Show, Don't Just Tell

Instead of saying "I am a hardworking person," describe a time when you worked through the night to finish a project or balanced multiple responsibilities under pressure. Concrete examples make your statement memorable and believable.

3. Focus on Why

Why this course? Why this university? Why now? You need to demonstrate a clear link between your past experiences, your current motivations, and your future career goals. Admissions committees want to see that you have a plan and that their program is the next logical step.

4. Structure Matters

  • Introduction: The hook and your main thesis.
  • Body Paragraphs: 2-3 paragraphs focusing on specific experiences or themes.
  • Conclusion: Summarize your main points and look forward to your future at the university.

5. Proofread, Proofread, Proofread

Spelling and grammar mistakes suggest a lack of care. Read your essay aloud, ask a mentor to review it, and use tools like Grammarly to catch any minor errors.
